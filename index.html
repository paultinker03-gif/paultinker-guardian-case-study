What are you really into?

An interactive campaign that turned people's Spotify listening into a musical footprint, a genre catalogue and a logo of their own, to get more of them using Discover Weekly.

Discover Weekly builds a personal playlist every Monday from what you listen to. Spotify wanted more people to know it existed, and needed a way to tell a wide audience that felt personal rather than like an advert. Spotify partnered with Guardian Labs, and I came up with the concept: an experience that asks one question, and lets your own listening history answer it. I was the designer and art director, from the idea through to the footprint system, the logo and every screen.

One question, answered by your listening

Log in with Spotify and the experience reads your listening history, finds your top five genres and gives you three things that belong to you alone.

  • A musical footprint Each of your genres brings one colour and one pattern, layered into an image of your own.
  • A bespoke genre catalogue A page for each genre: what it is, and the tracks of yours that put it there.
  • A personal logo and share card Both take on your footprint's colours, ready to post.

Three ways through

Most people arrived from a friend's share or a Guardian placement, logged in once, and left with something to post. Anyone could browse the genres without logging in, but only a logged-in visitor got a footprint.

A. Find out what you're into. The main journey: one login turns a listening history into a footprint, a catalogue and a card to share.

B. Share and bring friends in. Every share card is an invitation. A friend sees the post, lands on the same question, logs in and gets a footprint of their own, so the journey loops back to the start.

C. Browse without logging in. Genre pages work for everyone, but only your own top five genres show your footprint. Every other genre page is plain.

Five genres in, one footprint out

Every genre owns a colour and a pattern from a library of patterns, such as bubbles, shards, petals, swirls and ripples. Your number one genre sets the background, and the other four layer on top. The same five colours, in the same order, colour the words of your logo and the names in your catalogue, so everything you take away matches.

Results

Around 40,000 people visited over four weeks. What they were into also told Spotify which genres its listeners cared about most.
